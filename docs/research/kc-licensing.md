Research draft, not legal advice. Confirm with each jurisdiction before relying on it.

- **Companion data:** `dealer-prospects.csv` now has `license_regulator`, `license_number`, `license_status` and `license_checked_on` filled for all 20 rows. Where nothing was found, those fields are blank except `license_checked_on`, and `notes` gives the lookup outcome.
- **How to read this file:** a license is listed only when a regulator's lookup showed it. What a company says about its own licensing is marked self-reported. Insurance is "not publicly verifiable" unless a public source shows it.

## Short answer

- **Kansas:** no state plumbing license was found. Kansas statutes leave plumber licensing and plumbing permits to cities and counties (K.S.A. 12-1505, 12-1509). In Johnson County, cities such as Overland Park and Olathe require a **Johnson County Contractor License** for any permitted work. Plumbing is class **DP**. In Kansas City KS / Wyandotte County, the Unified Government requires a **master per trade** plus an occupational tax license.
- **Missouri:** no state-issued plumbing license was found. A 1939 statute (RSMo 341.010) requires a plumbing certificate in cities of 15,000 or more people, issued through local boards. In practice the city licenses: KCMO issues a **Plumbing Contractor** license, Independence requires a **Master Plumber** license for permitted work, and Lee's Summit requires a **contractor business license** backed by a master's certification.
- **Softener / RO / whole-home filter installs:** none of the sources found names water softeners or water-treatment equipment. In KCMO and Overland Park the rule text arguably covers connecting a softener at the main line. KCMO's plumbing scope includes "appliances connected" to potable water piping, and Overland Park's permit exemptions do not list it. That is an interpretation, not a stated rule, so every jurisdiction is marked **unclear**. Ask each building department this exact question: "Does installing a whole-home softener with a bypass at the main line need a plumbing permit and a licensed plumber?"

## What this means for the pilot

- The two cross-state plumbing companies on the priority list, **Dragonfly** and **JF Denney**, passed in both Johnson County and KCMO.
- The water-treatment specialists have no public contractor license on record in either lookup. That does not show they work unlicensed. Possible explanations: they subcontract the plumbing connection, a licensed plumber works under another company name, they work only where no license applies, or they treat softener installs as not needing a permit. Any of these needs a direct answer before a dealer agreement is signed, and asking requires owner approval for contact.
- If KCMO or Overland Park confirms that softener installs are plumbing work, a specialist without a license would need a licensed plumbing partner for installs in those cities.

## Unknowns

1. Whether a whole-home softener, RO unit or whole-home filter installation counts as plumbing work needing a permit or licensed plumber in **each** jurisdiction. No source names this equipment. Ask each building department.
2. Johnson County class DP scope text. The Definitions page on cls.jocogov.org did not return the class definitions, and jocogov.org's new-licensee page returned 403.
3. Olathe code text. olatheks.gov and municipal.codes blocked direct access, so the Olathe excerpt above is search-indexed text and not directly verified.
4. Current codified text of KCMO Chapter 18 and Overland Park Title 16. Municode, American Legal and the KCMO website blocked access. The ordinances cited (TMP-1174 from 2021, BC-3286 from 2020) may have been amended since.
5. Current Unified Government (KCK/Wyandotte) requirements. Its documents are dated 2010 and 2011, and it has no online license list.
6. Independence and Lee's Summit license status for every prospect. Independence has no online lookup, and the Lee's Summit lookup is behind a CAPTCHA.
7. Gladstone, Blue Springs, Greenwood, Leavenworth and other city licenses. Not checked.
8. Legal entity name, status and registered office for every prospect. The Kansas and Missouri Secretary of State searches were blocked from this environment. Run them manually in a browser.
9. Whether RSMo 341.090 to 341.220 (which defines "minor repairs" as excluding "the replacing or setting of any fixture") applies anywhere in the pilot area. Not established.
10. Insurance for every prospect. Not publicly verifiable. Request certificates naming the relevant city where required (Lee's Summit requires this).
11. Whether the KCMO license matches for MVP, the JoCo match for Moore (Wichita parent) and the Lutz records are the same businesses as the websites. Confirm with the company or the regulator.
